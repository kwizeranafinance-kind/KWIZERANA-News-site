Key Trends

  • The hashrate bear market is structural, and it is caused by a buyer rather than a ban. Roughly 150 EH/s is sidelined, difficulty is 15.1% lower than it began 2026, and the 2021 analogue fails because sites are being repurposed rather than relocated. The revenue maths forces it: AI hosting yields ~$1.29–1.65M per MW-year against ~$0.79–1.00M for hashing, and hashprice would need to roughly double to $60–70/PH/day to reach parity.
  • Land and interconnects have decoupled from hashboards as the real asset. Bitdeer paid $100M cash for empty land; Hut 8's leases are worth $19.6B against 704 MW. The tape agrees — HUT ~$89.61, IREN ~$43.72 and BTDR +4.3% on the day, while MARA, the purest large-cap hashrate play, fell 2.5%. J.P. Morgan's IREN December target of $24 now sits ~45% below spot.
  • A genuine but fragile margin reprieve, resting entirely on price. August's $1.008B was driven by a 24.95% BTC rally, not efficiency. Fees remain irrelevant at 0.70% of revenue, hashprice is still historically depressed, and the Sep 5 retarget of +1.58% mechanically claws back part of the relief. Miner outflows stayed limited, suggesting operators read this as a rally to hold through.

Ones to Watch

  • The Sep 5 difficulty retarget (+1.58%, ~19:30 UTC). Difficulty sits just 0.7% above the 2026 low and every recovery since June has been erased. A positive print confirms some of the ~150 EH/s is returning to chase the improved hashprice, immediately compressing the discount marginal miners are surviving on. A flat or negative print means capacity is not returning even at $38.86/PH/day — a far more bearish read on hashrate's structural floor, and a validation of Zagury's thesis.
  • Bitmain S23 XP Hyd. November shipping into a secondhand ASIC flood. The first sub-9 J/TH flagship (600 TH/s, 8.9 J/TH, ~$25.5/T) arrives exactly as Hyperscale, Keel and others dump decommissioned fleets and Bitmain runs a half-price $11/T promo on the 865 TH/s S23e U2H. If the S23 XP batches sell through, surviving miners are consolidating for a post-shakeout share grab; if they do not, the ASIC market becomes the leading indicator for the 2028 halving. Specs are reseller-sourced — verify against Bitmain directly.

Key Trends

  • The GENIUS yield ban is actively rewiring where dollars sit. August's ~$15B supply contraction was a migration, not stress — both USDT and USDC held peg throughout. Prohibiting licensed issuers from paying yield pushes capital into tokenised Treasuries and yield wrappers: total stablecoin cap is flat (+0.08% 7d) while USDe is +10% and RLUSD +53% on 30 days, and BUIDL/USYC/BENJI collectively hold ~$8B.   • The incumbents have stopped experimenting and started building. 21 global banks forming a joint issuer, BitGo bringing regulated custody to gold and real estate, Securitize listed on NYSE, and DTCC targeting an October commercial tokenisation launch.   • Collateral utilisation is the real signal, not RWA AUM. The utilisation split proves it: Janus Henderson JAAA at 97.94%, Re Protocol reUSD 97.76%, syrupUSDT 86.98%, OnRe 82.75% — against BUIDL at 0.64% and BENJI at 0.00% DeFi utilisation. Blue-chip tokenised Treasuries are parked capital; private credit and reinsurance are where tokenisation is actually being used.

Ones to Watch

  • GENIUS Act §3 comment deadline — October 19, 2026, effective date January 18, 2027. Final rules determine whether yield pass-through survives in any form and whether state frameworks are deemed substantially similar. If the ban is finalised as drafted, expect a second and larger migration out of pure stablecoins into BUIDL/USYC/BENJI-style products, and a re-rating of Ondo, Maple, Superstate and Centrifuge.

Watch alongside the NYSE/Securitize 24/7 tokenised-stock platform — if both ship in Q4, tokenised equities overtake private credit as the second-largest RWA category in 2027.
