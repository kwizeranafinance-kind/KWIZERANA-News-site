SIGNAL 01

Bitcoin clears its 50-week moving average for the first time in 45 weeks

Moved
Bitcoin closed the week ended Sep 20 above its 50-week moving average (~$78,115) for the first time in 45 weeks, up nearly 6% on the week and roughly 29% over the prior 35 days to ~$81,450 (Galaxy Research, via CoinDesk); live pricing at this brief's compile time (Sep 21, ~13:55 UTC, CoinGecko) puts BTC higher still at $85,557. Describe the move as roughly +5–6% on the day, +12% over the prior four sessions rather than one precise 24h percentage.
Matters
Galaxy Research found Bitcoin has reclaimed this level 13 times since 2011; in 11 of those instances the market did not go on to set a new low, and several reclaims preceded rallies ranging roughly 8x to ~600x from the prior cycle low. It's a real technical marker — not just a round number — that the bear phase since October 2025's $126,000 high may have run its course, though 2 of the 13 prior reclaims (Dec 2021, Mar 2022) failed and rolled over.
Next
Whether BTC holds above the ~$78,115 fifty-week average in the sessions ahead is the tell. Per Cryptonomist's technical model, the next resistance test is the daily pivot R1 near $86,583, with both daily RSI (71.58) and hourly RSI (84.75) already deep in overbought territory, and the Fear & Greed Index having jumped from 50 “Neutral” to 70 “Greed” in four days. A weekly close back below $78,115 would break the reclaim; a print above $86,583 would confirm it.

Moved

Circle Arc Studio turns plain-English prompts into full-stack blockchain apps — testable on Avalanche among nine chains
✓ Verified Cryptonomist · Sep 18, 2026 · source · Circle's own Arc announcement quoted directly; primary
Arc Studio is an AI coding agent that generates frontend, backend and smart-contract code from a plain-English prompt, with built-in Aave/Morpho/Uniswap composability and USDC payments/wallets/swaps/bridges baked in; generated apps can be tested across nine chains — Arbitrum, Arc, Avalanche, Base, Ethereum, Monad, OP Mainnet, Polygon and Unichain. $7.8M rsETH drained from a Gnosis Safe via a flawed Multicall authorization check; MEV bot “Yoink” recovers most of it
✓ Verified CoinDesk (Oliver Knight) · Sep 15, 2026 · source · independently corroborated by BlockSec, Blockaid, SlowMist, AstraSec and KelpDAO's own statement
A whitelisted Multicall helper-contract's authorization check let an attacker drain ~2,900 rsETH (~$7.8M) from a single Gnosis Safe; an MEV bot nicknamed “Yoink” front-ran the attacker's follow-up transaction and recovered roughly 2,882 of the 2,900 rsETH to a separate address.
A third-party helper-contract authorization bug, not a Safe or Kelp core flaw, drained the funds — and an MEV bot beating the attacker to the recovery is now a recurring pattern worth tracking as an informal incident-response layer.

Deprecated-but-still-live vault contracts remain a live attack surface long after a protocol has moved on — flash-loan share-price inflation keeps recurring against exactly this class of forgotten contract.

X sues two named defendants and unidentified operators over £207,384 in alleged Creator Revenue Sharing fraud by Bitcoin-focused accounts
✓ Verified Cryptonomist · Sep 21, 2026 · source · UK High Court filing (claim BL-2026-001161) reviewed directly; X's own Sep 20 public statement quoted
X Internet Unlimited Company and X Corp. filed suit Sep 17 in the Business and Property Courts of England and Wales against Vivek Kumar Sen, Zamyang Sherpa and “persons unknown,” alleging six Bitcoin-themed accounts (@Vivek4real_, @Bitcoin_Teddy, @saylordocs, @TrendingBitcoin, @Kalshibacktest, @PolyBackTest) coordinated posting, replies and reposts — in one case just 11 seconds apart — to fraudulently extract at least £207,384 from Creator Revenue Sharing before X suspended the accounts Aug 18. X is seeking the funds back plus damages, interest and at least £75,000 in investigation/remediation costs. No defense filing or judgment had been located as of Sep 21; the allegations are unproven in court.
A platform pursuing creator-payout fraud through civil litigation, not just account suspension, is a harder enforcement line than prior monetization-abuse cases — and it lands as X winds down the exact program (Creator Revenue Sharing, replaced Sep 8 by an impressions-based model) the defendants allegedly gamed.
Revolut hackers (“iamnotavillain”) demand $3M in Monero, threaten to sell 680 accounts' data to other criminal groups
◆ Single-source Financial Times, relayed by CoinDesk · Sep 16, 2026 (updated Sep 17) · source · ransom terms are the threat actor's own claim to the FT, unconfirmed by Revolut
A group calling itself “iamnotavillain” demanded 6,000 XMR (~$3M) within 24 hours, saying it used blockchain analysis to target Revolut accounts with significant crypto holdings; Revolut told CoinDesk it “has not received any direct contact or demand from the individuals or group making these claims.” The deadline has long since passed as of this edition with no confirmed payment or data leak reported.
Pairing verified KYC documents with bitcoin transaction history is a ready-made target list for extortion of named holders; that Revolut denies direct contact while the FT's reporting stands is exactly the kind of gap this brief flags rather than resolves.
Revolut discloses the underlying breach: passports, KYC selfies and full BTC histories exposed after an AI-assisted fake government request passed authorization checks
✓ Verified CoinDesk · Sep 12, 2026 · source · Revolut's own notice plus independent confirmation from on-chain investigator ZachXBT
Revolut's compliance team honoured a fraudulent government information request that had passed its own authentication checks, exposing passports, KYC selfies and full bitcoin transaction histories; Revolut says no funds were lost and its core systems were unaffected.
This is the root incident behind the ransom demand above — a compliance-process failure, not a code exploit — and it is the same playbook (fraudulent “official” requests defeating identity checks) worth watching across other custodial platforms.

A hacker minted 46.1 billion fake syBTC tokens from a 330-satoshi deposit via two chained Bitcoin Bridge bugs
