SIGNAL 02

Blockstream refused the ransom — and neither of this week's two big losses involved a stolen key

Moved
The Liquid attacker returned roughly 3,400 of ~4,000 BTC and kept 598.5 BTC (~$46M), then demanded 10% as a “bug bounty” in a plaintext on-chain OP_RETURN. Blockstream refused outright today, calling it theft rather than disclosure (The Block 07:21 ET, Unchained independently, plus Blockstream's own statement — primary). The root cause was an Elements range-proof verification caching flaw that let unbacked L-BTC be minted and pegged out through federation member SideSwap, taking the reserve from ~4,205 BTC to ~197 BTC. Separately and in the same week, an attacker used signed Symbiosis BridgeV2 transactions to mint unbacked synthetic bitcoin and cashed out only ~4.39 WBTC (~$336,000), leaving the rest outstanding.
Matters
Not one signing key was compromised in either incident. Both were validation failures that created unbacked synthetic supply against a real reserve — which means “the signers are honest and the HSMs are intact” has stopped being a sufficient security assertion for federated pegs and bridges, and the entire post-Ronin industry spend on key management would not have prevented either one. The second-order move is Blockstream's: by refusing publicly, it closes the negotiated-bounty off-ramp attackers have relied on since 2022, which pushes the residual into pure law-enforcement tracing and makes the next exploiter's calculus binary rather than negotiable.
Next
Two falsifiable tests, both live. Liquid peg-ins and peg-outs remain suspended with no published timeframe — Adam Back has pledged 1:1 L-BTC coverage without saying how the 598.5 BTC hole gets funded, so the thing to watch is whether pegs reopen after Blockstream absorbs the shortfall onto its own balance sheet or before, at less than 1:1.
