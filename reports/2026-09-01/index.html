Cronos restarts by rolling state back to before the Tectonic exploit
The Defiant / CryptoSlate · Aug 31 – Sep 1, 2026 · source
After roughly ten hours without a block, validators resumed production from block 90,896,189 at 23:49:01 UTC on Aug 30 with chain state restored to pre-attack. About $68.7M of the ~$75M remains frozen on Cronos; only ~$6M was bridged to Ethereum. RPC providers, explorers and bridges are still catching up, and Tectonic says a verified update follows its investigation.
A rollback is a hard fork by another name. Cronos just demonstrated that a small validator set can rewrite settled state to undo a loss — useful here, and exactly the property KWIZERANA should price as a risk on any chain it deploys treasury to.
More Markets on Flow EVM loses ~$9.3M to an Aave V3 e-mode style abuse
Cryptonomist / Blockaid · Aug 31, 2026 · source
The attacker combined an Ankr liquid-staking token with a capital-efficiency feature inherited from Aave V3's architecture, borrowing against correlated-asset assumptions the deployment never earned. Blockaid disclosed the incident; More Labs is the operator.
Forked code inherits forked assumptions. Aave V3's high-LTV modes are safe on Aave because Aave curates the asset list — a fork that does not re-curate is shipping a loaded gun.

Coldcard drain still 2026's single largest loss at $116M
TRM Labs · ongoing since Jul 30, 2026 · source
Roughly 1,816 BTC taken from 5,200+ addresses across four waves. Root cause is a March 2021 firmware release whose seed generation used weak software randomness, letting attackers reproduce keys. Stolen funds are pooling at a few addresses with minimal laundering.
Any seed generated on a Coldcard between March 2021 and the patch should be treated as compromised and rotated. This is a treasury action item, not a news item.

Mixer policy has settled: lawful privacy is defensible, operators are not immune
Cambridge Judge Business School · 2026 · source
US sanctions on Tornado Cash were lifted in 2025 after the Fifth Circuit held immutable contracts are not sanctionable property, but several mixer operators and developers have since faced criminal prosecution. Tornado Cash processed roughly $2.5B in 2025 even while sanctioned.
The line is now code versus conduct. Publishing privacy-preserving contracts is protected; operating a service that knowingly launders is not. Build on the code side of that line and document it.

Key Trends

  • Economics, not cryptography. Every material August loss came from oracle marks, governance quorums, correlated-asset modes or firmware randomness. The maths held; the configuration did not.
  • Hardware is back in the threat model. The Coldcard drain alone exceeds every DeFi hack of 2026 combined. Cold storage is a process, and firmware provenance is now part of it.
  • Privacy tooling has legal ground again. The Fifth Circuit ruling plus the 2025 delisting give compliant privacy infrastructure the clearest standing since 2022 — provided operators keep records.

Ones to Watch

  • Coldcard attribution. Four waves, one month, no named actor, minimal laundering. If forensics lands on a state group, firmware supply-chain rules follow fast and hit every hardware vendor.
  • Whether any major lender delists thin-float collateral. The fix after Tectonic and More Markets is cheap and obvious. Watch who ships a curated asset list versus who only publishes a post-mortem.

Morpho's $175M at up to $2B remains the year's benchmark DeFi round
CoinDesk / Fortune · June 2026 · source
Co-led by a16z crypto, Paradigm and Ribbit, with Apollo, Circle Ventures, VanEck and Ledger Cathay participating. Morpho holds more than $11B in deposits and serves Galaxy, Anchorage and Bitwise. a16z crypto closed a $2.2B fifth fund in May.

Key Trends

  • Fewer, larger, more regulated. Deal count down 46% year-over-year while quarterly dollars nearly doubled Q1 to Q2. The capital that remains wants compliance surface.

  • Infrastructure beats applications, again. Clearing, payments, tax and credit rails took the year's largest cheques. No one is funding another AMM.
