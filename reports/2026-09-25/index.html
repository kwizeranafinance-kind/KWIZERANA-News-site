SIGNAL 01

Bitget loses $351.6M in a hot-wallet breach — 2026's largest exchange hack, North Korea suspected

Moved
Bitget confirmed $351.6M left its hot and warm wallets on Sep 24 after attackers compromised the exchange's backend transaction-authorization logic rather than stealing private keys, per CEO Gracy Chen; cold wallets were unaffected and withdrawals were frozen. Bloomberg, CoinDesk, CNBC and Bitcoin Magazine independently confirm the figure and timeline; on-chain tracing by Arkham Intelligence and Hacken corroborates the outflows.
Matters
This overtakes Sep 7's Liquid Network exploit ($319–340M) as 2026's largest single crypto hack, and the attack vector matters more than the size: spoofing the signing/authorization layer defeats standard cold-storage key segregation, so an exchange's transaction-approval pipeline — not just where keys sit — is now the binding security question. CryptoSlate estimates the loss could drain roughly 76% of Bitget's $464M User Protection Fund.
Next
Watch for Bitget's promised technical post-mortem and for a named security firm — not just Bitget's own statement — to independently confirm the North Korea attribution Chen has claimed from IP/VPN pattern-matching and one researcher's address clustering. KelpDAO sues LayerZero and CEO Bryan Pellegrino over the $292M rsETH exploit
✓ Verified CoinDesk & The Block · Sep 25, 2026 · source · corroborated independently by The Block and CryptoTimes
Evercrest Technologies, KelpDAO's operator, filed suit in the BC Supreme Court (Vancouver, file #267169) alleging LayerZero Labs reviewed and endorsed the 1-of-1 verifier configuration on the Unichain bridge that a North Korea-linked attacker used to drain 116,500 rsETH (about $292M) on April 18, 2026. Why it matters: this is the first major test of whether a bridge-infrastructure provider can be held liable for endorsing an insecure configuration rather than the hacked protocol alone — a precedent that could reshape how infra providers document security guidance. Pellegrino called the claims “meritless.”

Bitget loses $351.6M in hot/warm-wallet breach, suspects North Korea
✓ Verified Bloomberg, CoinDesk, CNBC, Bitcoin Magazine · Sep 24–25, 2026 · source · corroborated by CNBC
Attackers compromised Bitget's backend wallet-authorization logic — spoofing signed transaction data rather than stealing private keys — and moved $351.6M out of hot and warm wallets on Sep 24; cold wallets were unaffected and withdrawals were frozen. CEO Gracy Chen points to North Korea via IP/VPN pattern-matching and one researcher's address clustering, though no named security firm has independently confirmed that attribution yet. Why it matters: an authorization-layer compromise defeats standard cold-storage key segregation, and CryptoSlate estimates the loss could drain about 76% of Bitget's $464M User Protection Fund.
Duelbits crypto casino hacked, hot wallets drained across four chains
✓ Verified CoinDesk, cybersecuritynews.com, GBHackers · Sep 24, 2026 · source
Duelbits' hot wallets on Ethereum, BNB Chain, Tron and Bitcoin were drained and the platform taken offline; about $6M (2,234 ETH) sits unmoved in one address. Why it matters: multiple outlets note this isn't Duelbits' first hot-wallet breach, raising repeat-operational-security questions for crypto casinos specifically, a subsector with weaker audit and disclosure norms than DeFi protocols or major exchanges.
📐 Methodology Loss figures disagree by more than the size of some hacks entirely: Scam Sniffer and Duelbits' own co-founder put it at about $7M; PeckShield's itemized alert lists 836 ETH, 1.146M USDT, 209 BNB, 96,805 USDC, 12.398B SHIB and 31,515 DAI, which The Crypto Basic notes sums to only about $3.74M against PeckShield's own $4.3M headline. Report the range as $4.3M–$7M and name both firms; do not pick one.
Bitcoin privacy proposal adds ZK shielded transfers without a soft fork
✓ Verified crypto.news, KuCoin, Bitbase · Sep 25, 2026 · source
Researchers at Alloc Init (Clara Shikhelman, Mikhail Komarov, Aleksei Moskvin) published a design treating Bitcoin as a neutral publication/ordering layer while off-chain indexers verify ZK proofs for Zcash-style shielded transfers, changing no consensus rules. Why it matters: it sidesteps the years-long deadlock over privacy-adding soft forks by moving trust-minimization to an indexer layer — but critics flag a thin initial anonymity set and no quantum resistance, and the core BTC-in/BTC-out bridging mechanism is still unpublished.
This Week (Sep 18–25)
SEC Commissioner Peirce backs zero-knowledge proofs for KYC compliance
✓ Verified crypto.news, en.coin-turk.com, Bitcoin Foundation · Sep 23–25, 2026 · source
Speaking at SIFMA's Digital Assets Conference on Sep 23, in her penultimate week as SEC commissioner, Hester Peirce argued regulators should move from prescriptive data collection toward attribute-based ZK verification for KYC, and noted the SEC Crypto Task Force met with Aztec Labs on similar tech in July. Why it matters: it's the clearest public signal yet that a sitting US securities regulator sees privacy-preserving cryptography as compliance-compatible, not compliance-evading — though she stressed these are personal, not agency, views.
Privacy coins rally as a Wall Street rotation narrative builds
✓ Verified 24/7 Wall St., news.bitcoin.com · Sep 21, 2026 · source · “$24.54B in 5 months” figure single-source, unconfirmed

Why it matters: a sustained institutional narrative toward privacy coins would run counter to the exchange-delisting trend of the past two years, suggesting some institutions now see privacy tech and compliance as reconcilable rather than opposed.
Q3 2026 crypto hack losses fall 37% to $509M as tactics shift to wallets
✓ Verified Cointelegraph, citing CertiK & Hacken · week of Sep 18–25, 2026 · source
Total Q3 hack losses fell to $509M from $803M in Q2 and over $1.7B in Q1; code-vulnerability losses dropped from $272M to $78M while attackers shifted toward phishing and social-engineering compromises of multisig and hot wallets, with North Korean units responsible for roughly half of Q3 theft. Why it matters: this exactly matches this week's two headline incidents (Bitget, Duelbits) — smart-contract audits are no longer the binding security constraint; wallet-signing infrastructure and access control are.

Key Trends

  • Attackers have moved up the stack, from code to authorization logic — this week's two headline incidents (Bitget, Duelbits) were both wallet/backend-authorization compromises, not contract bugs, matching the Q3 CertiK/Hacken data.
  • Privacy tech is having a regulatory-legitimacy moment, not just a market one — a sitting SEC commissioner endorsing ZK-KYC, a serious Bitcoin-native shielded-transfer proposal, and a reported institutional rotation into ZEC/XMR all landed the same week.
  • North Korea attribution is becoming the reflexive first theory for any large exchange hack — Bitget's CEO reached for it within hours, before any named security firm's independent confirmation.

✓ Verified Federal Reserve (primary), The Block, CoinDesk, PYMNTS · Sep 24, 2026 · source
The Fed issued two proposed rules under the GENIUS Act: one requiring board-supervised issuers to fully back tokens with T-bills/HQLA plus standardized capital and risk-management standards, the other creating a tailored bank application process to issue stablecoins; a 60-day comment period follows Federal Register publication.
