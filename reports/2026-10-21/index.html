SIGNAL 03

The OCC finalized the first GENIUS Act rule, effective Jan 18, 2027, dropping the presumption that affiliate stablecoin rewards breach the yield ban

Moved
The OCC (The Block, The Crypto Times, Oct 21) became the first federal banking regulator to finalize its GENIUS Act rule, effective Jan 18, 2027, covering national banks, federal savings associations, nonbank applicants and foreign issuers, with waivers for legacy issuers capped at 12 months. The final text drops the proposed presumption that affiliate or third-party rewards violate the yield prohibition. The Fed, FDIC and Treasury are still at proposal stage. Against it, DefiLlama’s stablecoin sum is $304.19B (USDT $182.73B, USDC $72.17B) and Avalanche holds $1.39B of it.
Matters
Rewards on stablecoins held at exchanges are now a case-by-case review rather than an outright bar, so the Coinbase-versus-community-banks fight over yield continues instead of ending, and the OCC chose to act while CLARITY is stalled after the 49–50 cloture vote on Sept 15. For Avalanche, whose chain stablecoins are the metric Kris watches, the compliance perimeter for US-issued dollars is now defined from Jan 18, 2027. The OCC’s own final-rule release was not found (the link in search was the proposal), so the details rest on two secondary outlets; the Fed, FDIC and Treasury rules are not final.
Next
Whether the Fed or FDIC finalizes its own GENIUS rule by Nov 30 (neither had as of Oct 21); none by then would leave the OCC alone 49 days before the Jan 18 effective date. Coinbase’s EEA withdrawal date for non-MiCA stablecoins is Oct 30, and ESMA’s exit deadline is Jan 8, 2027.
