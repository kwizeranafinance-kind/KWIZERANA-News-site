SIGNAL 01

FinCEN withdrew both its 2020 unhosted-wallet rule and its 2023 mixer designation on Oct 5, the same day the CFTC proposed its own leveraged-crypto framework

Moved
Treasury's FinCEN withdrew two proposals on Oct 5: the Dec 2020 unhosted-wallet rule (record-keeping above $3,000, reporting above $10,000; Coin Republic cites 7,500+ comments) and the Oct 2023 rule that would have named international mixing a primary money-laundering concern (Decrypt, Coin Republic, Blockonomi). Separately the CFTC proposed Regulation CTX and CAM for leveraged retail crypto trading (The Block, Oct 5).
Matters
The one US federal threat of mandatory surveillance of self-custody wallets is gone, and the rulemaking that remains is moving to agencies while the CLARITY cloture vote sits at 49-50 from Sep 15 (bit.com, single source). Coin Center warns the underlying statutory authority survives and existing BSA duties are untouched, so this is a withdrawal of two proposals, not a safe harbour. The Federal Register or FinCEN primary notice was not opened; the tier rests on three independent outlets.
Next
Federal Register publication of the two withdrawal notices, and any new FinCEN or Treasury proposal naming mixers or self-custody before Dec 31: one would falsify the retreat reading. The SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets comment period closes Oct 20 (bit.com, single source).
