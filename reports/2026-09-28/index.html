SIGNAL 01

Bitget begins phased withdrawal resumption as THORChain refuses to blacklist the hacker's wallets

Moved
Bitget started a phased withdrawal resumption on Sep 28 (BTC first, ETH and other chains Sep 29, USDT Sep 30, full restoration Oct 2) following the Sep 24–25 breach of its wallet-approval backend, with the loss tally now widely reported at $387.5M (up from an initial $351.6M as more drained wallets were identified). Separately, THORChain publicly rejected CEO Gracy Chen's request to block hacker-linked addresses, saying its halt mechanism protects the protocol as a whole rather than freezing individual wallets and framing itself as permissionless by design; GoPlus Security says roughly $8.5M in BTC and $43M in XRP tied to the hack have already routed through THORChain. Circle and Tether have frozen only $318,000 of the total so far.
Matters
THORChain's refusal reopens the “is a permissionless protocol a laundering rail” debate that Tornado Cash first forced into the open — and unlike a sanctioned mixer, THORChain has no single entity for regulators to pressure, so the practical response shifts to exchanges geoblocking or delisting RUNE, or validators acting unilaterally, rather than protocol governance freezing funds. It also confirms the freeze playbook's limits: the ~63,000 ETH and the XRP the attacker holds sit almost entirely outside any issuer's reach, leaving stablecoin freezes as a rounding error against the total.
Next
Sep 29's ETH/other-chain withdrawal resumption and Sep 30's USDT resumption are the next tests of whether Bitget's phased plan holds without a further loss revision; full restoration is targeted for Oct 2. Separately, watch whether THORChain governance takes any coordinated validator action on the flagged addresses, or whether a second forensics firm (Chainalysis, Elliptic) independently confirms GoPlus Security's routing figures.
