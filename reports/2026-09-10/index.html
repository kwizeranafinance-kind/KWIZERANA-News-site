SIGNAL 02

Wrapped bitcoin broke three times in five days — and not one of them was a stolen key

Moved
Three separate bitcoin-wrapper failures inside five days. Liquid Network: ~$319M (~4,000 BTC) minted as unbacked L-BTC through a range-proof cache flaw in Elements and pegged out via SideSwap in 36 minutes, 13:53→14:28 UTC on Sep 6 (TRM Labs, primary). 3,400 BTC came back on Sep 7; 598.5 BTC (~$47M) is still out, and on Sep 10 the attackers dropped the “white hat” framing entirely, switching to plaintext OP_RETURN to demand “10% using your own money as bug bounty.” Separately, a Nomic double-spend compromised 39.84 nBTC — ~36% of alloyed-BTC backing on Osmosis — and an emergency chain upgrade froze 22.65 BTC (Sep 9).
Matters
Every one of these was a validation failure, not a credential failure — Liquid's 11-of-15 HSM quorum signed correctly and was bypassed upstream of signing, which means the key-management hardening the industry has spent its entire post-Ronin budget on would not have stopped any of them. The second-order problem is the aggregation design: allBTC bundles multiple bridged BTC variants, so holders who never chose Nomic exposure got it anyway, and the same is true of anyone holding a basket wrapper today. TRM's reserve arithmetic implies L-BTC is running at roughly 86% backing (~3,597 BTC against ~4,200 L-BTC outstanding).
Next
Two dated, falsifiable tests. Blockstream's staged restart — resume block production, replay verified-valid transactions, reopen pegs only after state restoration — has no published date and no announced remedy for the ~86% backing ratio; a restart at less than 1:1 would be the first time a sidechain federation absorbs a shortfall in public, and that is the thing to watch for, not the price. Second, the Osmosis governance vote to seize the frozen 22.65 BTC and top up the gap from the community pool: if it passes, a Cosmos DAO will have voted to confiscate an attacker's address, and that becomes the template every IBC chain reaches for next time.
