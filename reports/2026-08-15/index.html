Last 7 Days
Coldcard seed-entropy flaw traced to firmware shipped since March 2021
TechCrunch / Bloomberg · Aug 3–4, 2026 · read
Rather than trusting the hardware RNG alone, the flawed firmware hashed the device seed together with every dice roll a user entered, shrinking the effective keyspace. Attacks began July 30; 1,000+ addresses swept in 41 minutes. Affected users must generate a new seed and migrate.
A five-year-dormant bug in the most security-maximalist product category in crypto. "Cold storage" is not a security model on its own — firmware provenance is.
Zcash Ironwood upgrade live — supply verifiability plus quantum-resistant records
Cointelegraph Magazine · activated July 2026 · read
Ironwood replaces the Orchard shielded pool after a four-year counterfeiting vulnerability was patched in June, and lets users independently verify circulating supply without breaking shielding.
The privacy stack is converging on "auditable privacy" — provable supply integrity without deanonymisation. That's the design KWIZERANA's privacy pillar should track.
Ethereum Foundation privacy team and institutional ZK push define "pragmatic privacy" 2026
insights4vc / Cointelegraph · week of Aug 10 · read
A dedicated EF privacy group, Canton's institutional shielding, and Coinbase lobbying for updated rules are converging on compliant privacy rather than absolute anonymity.
Post-Storm verdict, the viable privacy business is selective disclosure. Absolute-anonymity tooling now carries founder liability.

Key Trends

  • The attack surface moved off-chain. Coldcard (firmware), Trezor (3PL logistics), Metabase (vendor SaaS) — three incidents in two weeks, none of them a smart-contract bug.
  • Dormant vulnerabilities are the real tail risk. Coldcard's flaw sat five years; Zcash's Orchard bug sat four. Audits certify a snapshot, not a lifetime.
  • Privacy is professionalising into selective disclosure. Zcash Ironwood, EF's privacy team and Canton all target provable correctness with confidentiality, not anonymity maximalism.

Ones to Watch

  • Third-party vendor risk in crypto supply chains. ShipMonk and Metabase are neither crypto companies nor audited as such, yet they held the customer list for a hardware-wallet vendor. Expect more of this class of breach.
  • Whether Coldcard-stolen BTC starts moving. $130M sitting still is the single most watchable forensics thread of the quarter.
